# Movers4Less — working price book and operating assumptions

**Internal planning document · research date: 7 November 2026 · UK / London-first concept**

This is a model for testing the business idea. It is not an approved tariff, quote, insurance statement or offer to customers. No service is currently operating and no bookings are being accepted.

## 1. The business proposition

The proposed advantage is not simply “the cheapest removals company”. The proposition is **clear, modular and reasonably priced moving help**, so customers can pay for the work they actually need and are less likely to give away good furniture because moving it feels unaffordable.

The model should offer:
- Whole-home removals with crew and vehicle matched to volume.
- Small moves and man-and-van work.
- Self-pack materials, fragile-only packing and full packing as separate options.
- Careful handling of bulky items, with specialist equipment hired only when justified.
- Clear itemised quotes showing crew, vehicle, materials, access and any extra charges.

Do not promise instant fixed prices from bedroom count alone. The final price must be based on inventory, access, distance, vehicle capacity, time and any special handling.

## 4. Labour model and crew planning

### Pay assumptions

The statutory National Living Wage for workers aged 21+ is £12.71/hour from 1 April 2026. That is the legal floor, not a sensible recruitment target for reliable, physically demanding removals work.

Initial modelling assumption:
- Basic mover pay: **£14.50–£16.00 gross per paid hour**.
- Use £14.50/hour as the sample cost model, then test at £16/hour.
- Allow for paid holiday (most workers are entitled to 5.6 weeks), employer National Insurance, applicable workplace pension contributions, payroll costs, training, PPE, sickness/absence and non-productive paid time.
- Model a **loaded productive labour cost of roughly £19–£21 per worker-hour**. This is an internal planning allowance, not a payroll quote.

For 2026–27, employer Class 1 National Insurance is 15% above the £5,000 annual secondary threshold. Actual liability depends on employee earnings and any available reliefs. A payroll accountant should model the actual workforce and Employment Allowance position.

Do not base the operating model on sham self-employment or unpaid travel/setup time. Confirm employment status and pay rules before engaging regular movers.

### Crew planning guide

| Job type | Starting crew assumption | Typical planning window* |
|---|---|---|
| Few items / simple collection | 1 mover where safe; 2 for heavier pieces | 2–4 hours |
| Studio / small flat | 2 movers | 3–5 hours |
| 1-bedroom home | 2 movers | 4–6 hours |
| 2-bedroom home | 2–3 movers | 5–8 hours |
| 3-bedroom home | 3 movers; extra vehicle if volume requires | 7–10 hours |
| 4-bedroom home | 4 movers and possibly 2 vans | Full day |
| 5+ bedroom / complex | Survey and job plan | Survey-led |

*Times are planning placeholders, not promised completion times. Volume, stairs, long carries, parking, distance, lift bookings, disassembly and multiple trips can change them substantially.

A minimum of two movers should be the normal starting point for whole-home removals and heavy furniture. One mover is appropriate only for small, manageable items or jobs where the customer can safely assist and the risk assessment permits it.

## 5. Vehicle and travel assumptions

### Start asset-light

For the initial pilot, compare short-term hire with leasing or buying. Avoid financing a van before the business has repeatable demand. Hire a suitable vehicle per confirmed job and include the full cost in the job costing.

Public hire examples reviewed:
- Luton with tail lift from about £125/day from one rental listing.
- Another dedicated Luton hire provider advertises from about £199/day, with 100 miles/day included.
- Check the real daily price for the exact dates, insurance excess, mileage allowance, additional driver, deposit, fuel, delivery and cancellation rules.

Draft costing allowance:
- Standard van: obtain actual quotes; do not assume a low headline rate.
- Luton with tail lift: £125–£199/day as an initial research band.
- Fuel: use the current local diesel price and actual vehicle consumption; do not fix a fuel allowance for the year.
- Parking, permits, tolls and congestion charges: disclose actual costs or agreed allowances before the customer accepts.

UK reported average diesel was about £1.976/litre on 7 November 2026. Fuel volatility makes regular price review essential.

## 6. Specialist equipment and when to hire it

Do not buy expensive equipment for an unproven business. Hire per job, or refer the job to a specialist partner. Equipment must be appropriate for the load, route, staircase, ground conditions and safe working limit. Operators need training and a documented method of work.

| Equipment | Potential use | Research cost / approach |
|---|---|---|
| Tail-lift Luton van | Appliances, heavy cartons, furniture loading | Usually part of vehicle hire; compare £125–£199/day examples. |
| Manual stair-climbing trolley | Boxes and manageable loads on stairs | From about £13/day ex VAT on a weekly-rate listing. |
| Powered stair climber, 190–310kg class | White goods and selected heavy/awkward items | Roughly £50–£70/day ex VAT on a public hire listing; delivery and hire minimums vary. |
| Higher-capacity powered stair climber | Heavy commercial equipment, safes or large loads within rated limits | Roughly £70/day ex VAT or more in public hire examples; verify the exact model and rate. |
| Furniture dolly, piano skates, straps, ramps, floor protection | Routine handling and protection | Purchase or hire only after specifying load and safe working limit. |
| External furniture hoist / platform lift | Sofa or large furniture that cannot pass through stairs or doorways | London guide indicates roughly £150–£250 for a two-hour operator booking, plus parking/permit costs. Verify by supplier quote. |
| Forklift | Palletised commercial moves at suitable premises | Not a normal residential-removals tool. Hire only for a site-specific job with a suitable surface, safe exclusion zone, competent operator and any required site arrangements. |

The phrase “lift forks” has been interpreted as lifting equipment generally. A forklift should not be used as a substitute for a furniture hoist or stair climber on ordinary domestic moves.

Specialist equipment costs should be passed through transparently, plus a reasonable coordination/handling charge where appropriate. Do not promise specialist lifting services until operators, equipment, method statements, insurance and risk assessments are verified.

These are deliberately simple scenarios showing why the business must not set prices from competitor marketing alone. The figures are planning assumptions, not supplier quotations.

| Parking / road costs | £15–£35 | £20–£35 | £25–£40 |
| Materials / consumables | £10–£20 | £15–£25 | £20–£35 |

The table demonstrates that the lower end of each selling range is only viable for a straightforward, efficient job. If the real crew duration or vehicle hire cost rises, the price must rise. Do not accept a loss-making job simply to advertise a low “from” price.

Track actual time and cost after every pilot move. Reprice if the gross contribution does not cover fixed overhead, insurance, marketing, admin, cancellations, vehicle downtime, damage excesses and owner time.

## 8. Pricing rules and exclusions to settle before launch

1. Inventory and survey: quote on the actual volume and access, not bedroom count alone.
2. Minimum booking: set a minimum duration and define the clock start/end.
3. Distance: define the included route/mileage and how return travel or long-distance routes are handled.
4. Access: document stairs, lift, long carry, parking, loading restrictions and walking distance; charge only when the extra time/cost is justified and disclosed.
5. Waiting time: disclose the included allowance and rate for delays outside the mover's control.
6. Packing: distinguish labour from material costs and clarify who packs fragile/high-value items.
7. Dismantling: list what is included; exclude complex installations, electrical disconnections and wall-mounted items unless properly qualified.
8. Specialist items: require assessment and an explicit written scope.
9. Cancellation, date changes, deposit and payment: take UK consumer law advice and provide clear written terms before accepting money.
10. VAT: the general compulsory registration threshold is taxable turnover above £90,000. The actual tax position must be reviewed before prices are published as VAT-inclusive or VAT-exclusive.

## 9. Insurance and compliance gates

Before the first paid move, obtain broker advice and written cover suitable for the actual removals activities. At minimum, review:
- Commercial motor insurance that permits the actual business use and hire/reward activity.
- Goods-in-transit cover for customers' belongings, including limits per vehicle/per item, packing exclusions, unattended vehicle conditions and loading/unloading.
- Public liability cover for property damage and injury to third parties.
- Employers' liability cover if staff are employed. The UK minimum cover is £5 million in most cases.
- Equipment cover and storage/warehouse cover only if those services are actually provided.
- Conditions of carriage, inventory, claims, complaints, customer terms and data privacy.
- Health and safety risk assessments, manual handling training, vehicle checks, driver licensing and safe load/payload limits.
- Operator licensing rules for vehicle plated weight and any trailer/international work. Many 3.5-tonne vans are below the domestic threshold, but check the exact plated/unladen weight and journey type.

Do not advertise “fully insured”, “trained specialists”, “same-day” or any accreditation unless evidenced and current.
